    ## How Many Screen Printing Methods Do We Use?

    #description
    We use as many as 5 methods, giving you a wide variety of shirt designs with a refined, beautiful finish — prints that are truly one of a kind and thoroughly modern.
    :::

    :::card
    ### Silkscreen Printing
    Silkscreen printing is a method well suited to large quantities of shirts with designs that need little fine detail, especially simple graphics. The process starts with creating the design and separating the colors, then printing them out as films.

    Next, the screens are prepared for printing. Each screen is a rectangular wooden frame stretched with mesh that carries the stencil of the design. The screen is placed on the material to be printed and ink is squeegeed through it. One screen prints one color, so you need as many screens as the number of colors in the design.

    This makes it ideal for wholesale apparel production — high volumes with relatively simple designs and few colors. There are several options to choose from, including discharge (soaked-in) printing, puff (raised) printing, plastisol (rubber) printing and foil printing, so you can pick the silkscreen method that suits your needs.
    :::

    :::card
    ### DTG (Direct to Garment) Printing
    DTG prints designs and colors directly onto the fabric with a printer. It works best on cotton and is suitable for both large and small quantities. Because it uses a digital printer, the results are sharp and highly detailed, and it can print any color.

    This removes the usual limits on logo colors and shirt colors, since it prints any shade on both dark and light shirts. The cost per piece depends on quantity; for large runs the total cost is higher, and it can only be printed on cotton fabric.
    :::

    :::card
    ### Sublimation Printing
    Sublimation is a printing method with unlimited colors. It is used on light-colored fabrics and works for any print quantity, making it suitable for both large and small orders.

    Sublimation is typically used on polyester. The design is first printed onto transfer paper, then applied to the fabric with an iron or heat press, which causes the ink to sublimate from the paper and bond with the fibers, so the color penetrates into the fabric.

    It is ideal for designs with unlimited colors, letting you create beautiful shirts in a wide range of colors, and the shirts are soft and comfortable to wear. However, it cannot be used on cotton fabric or dark-colored shirts.
    :::

    :::card
    ### Flex Printing
    Flex printing is used together with a flex (vinyl) cutting machine and is suitable for any print quantity. The design is created on a computer, cut with the flex cutter, and then applied with a heat press so the material bonds to the fabric. It is available in single color or unlimited colors, in two types:
    - Cut flex

    Solid colors, but with special textures available, such as glitter and velvet (flock).
    - Printed flex

    Can be printed with all kinds of designs and then die-cut. Flex work is quick, looks great on the fabric and needs no screens, but the print size is limited and the material is not breathable. For large quantities, however, it can cost less than silkscreen.
    :::

    :::card
    ### DTF (Direct to Film) / DFT (Digital Film Transfer) Printing
    DTF printing is ideal for designs with unlimited colors, works on many fabric types, and suits both large and small quantities. The design is printed onto a film with inks made specifically for textiles; the machine applies adhesive powder onto the white ink layer and cures it, then the film is placed on the garment and heat-pressed. It is a very convenient method, and it works on many fabrics, including cotton, polyester, TC, TK and nylon — with a wide range of colors on every one of them.
    :::
