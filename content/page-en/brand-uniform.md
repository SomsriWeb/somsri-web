FAQ — Private Label Apparel Manufacturing

#title
The most common questions before ordering branded apparel

#item-1-question
What is the minimum order for branded apparel?

#item-1-answer
The minimum is 150 pieces per design. If you need a smaller quantity, feel free to message our team to discuss the details.

#item-2-question
How long does branded apparel production take?

#item-2-answer
Standard production time is 14 – 21 days from design confirmation and deposit payment, depending on the complexity of the design and the order quantity.

#item-3-question
What fabrics are available for branded apparel?

#item-3-answer
We offer more than 50 fabrics, including Cotton Comb, Supersoft, TC and Dry Feel. Our team can recommend the right fabric for your budget and brand style.

#item-4-question
Is embroidery or screen printing better for branded apparel?

#item-4-answer
It depends on your budget and the image you want. Embroidery feels more premium and lasts longer, while screen printing suits multi-color or highly detailed artwork. Our team can recommend what fits your brand best.

#item-5-question
Can I order branded apparel online without visiting the factory?

#item-5-answer
Yes. Every step can be done online, from design consultation and fabric selection to reviewing samples before mass production. There is no need to visit the factory.

#item-6-question
What does the 90-day guarantee on branded apparel cover?

#item-6-answer
It covers sewing, printing and embroidery issues caused by factory errors, such as loose seams, peeling prints or patterns that deviate from the agreed design.

Insights on branded apparel manufacturing

#title
Private Label Apparel Manufacturing: How to Choose a Factory You Can Count On

#description
Choosing a private label apparel manufacturer should not come down to price per piece alone. Fabric, fit, seam finishing and print quality all shape the experience your customers have.

**Choose materials that match your retail price and brand image**\
Affordable lines may use CVC or TC fabrics, which are durable and easy to care for, while brands aiming for a premium image can choose Cotton Interlock or Heavy Cotton for a dense, structured feel. Your manufacturing team should help you balance quality, cost and brand margin.
